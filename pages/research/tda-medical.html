TDA for Medical Data

Topological Data Analysis for BRCA Breast Cancer Genomics

Overview

Modern medical datasets — particularly genomic data — are characterized by extremely high dimensionality, complex correlations, and subtle structural patterns that conventional statistical methods often fail to capture. Topological Data Analysis (TDA) offers a principled framework for extracting shape-based features from such data, revealing topological structures (connected components, loops, voids) that are invariant to noise and metric choice.

This research applies TDA to BRCA breast cancer datasets with the goal of discovering novel biomarkers and hidden topological structures that conventional univariate or correlation-based analyses cannot identify. By treating gene expression profiles as point clouds in high-dimensional space, we can uncover persistent features that correspond to biologically meaningful subtypes and progression patterns.

Why TDA for Cancer Genomics?

Traditional statistical pipelines (PCA, clustering, differential-expression tests) reduce data to first/second-order statistics and assume Euclidean structure. However, gene-expression manifolds are often curved and contain non-trivial topological features. TDA, through tools such as persistent homology and the Mapper algorithm, captures multiscale features that survive across resolutions — features which are robust to noise and reproducible across cohorts.

For breast cancer specifically, BRCA1/BRCA2 mutation carriers exhibit complex molecular subtypes with overlapping gene-expression signatures. We hypothesize that topological features may help distinguish these subtypes more reliably than purely linear methods, leading to more accurate prognosis and treatment-response predictions.

Methodology

  • Data: BRCA gene-expression and clinical data from TCGA (The Cancer Genome Atlas) and supplementary public repositories.
  • Persistent Homology: Compute persistence diagrams across multiple filtration parameters to identify stable topological features (H0, H1, H2).
  • Mapper Algorithm: Generate topological summaries (graph representations) of the data, revealing branching structures that may correspond to subtype evolution.
  • Feature Engineering: Convert topological summaries into machine-learnable features (persistence images, persistence landscapes) for downstream classification.
  • Validation: Cross-validate with known molecular subtypes (Luminal A/B, HER2-enriched, Basal-like) and compare against conventional baselines.

Goals & Expected Impact

The primary goal is to discover novel variables and biomarkers in breast cancer genomics that traditional statistical methods miss. By identifying topologically distinct patient clusters, we aim to contribute to more personalized treatment strategies and improved prognostic accuracy.

This work is conducted in collaboration with Permillion, a medical AI startup focused on data-driven oncology research. Findings will be evaluated for clinical relevance and potential integration into computational pipelines for early diagnosis.

Tools & Frameworks

Persistent homology computation: GUDHI, Ripser, scikit-tda · Mapper analysis: KeplerMapper, Giotto-tda · Standard ML: PyTorch, scikit-learn · Genomics: Bioconductor, TCGA APIs.
