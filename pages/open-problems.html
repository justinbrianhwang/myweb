Autonomous Driving

OP-13

Can autonomous vehicles recognize situations they have never seen before?

Looking for Collaborators

Autonomous models work well in environments resembling their training data, but real roads constantly present the unseen — unusual construction zones, rare objects, abnormal driver behavior, extreme weather. The real question goes beyond "can we detect OOD?" to: can an autonomous system recognize that it does not understand the current situation — and react safely?

Autonomous DrivingOOD DetectionTrustworthy AI
OP-14

Can we predict dangerous driving scenarios before they happen?

Looking for Collaborators

Beyond what perception currently sees, can we foresee a dangerous situation seconds ahead? For example, judging a pedestrian's crossing intent from posture, gaze, and heading before they step onto the road, or predicting a sudden lane change from subtle vehicle motion. The core: can an autonomous vehicle detect the early signatures of an accident before the dangerous event actually occurs?

Autonomous DrivingTrajectory PredictionRisk Modeling
OP-15

How can autonomous driving systems remain reliable when sensors fail?

Looking for Collaborators

In real vehicles, sensors are not guaranteed healthy — cameras get occluded, LiDAR degrades in bad weather, GPS drifts. Yet typical sensor fusion largely assumes every modality arrives intact. The question: can an autonomous system understand which sensors are trustworthy — and keep driving safely when some are not?

Quantum Communication (BB84)

OP-16

How robust is BB84 under realistic noise and imperfect hardware?

Looking for Collaborators

BB84 offers strong security under ideal conditions, but real systems carry non-idealities — photon loss, detector noise, imperfect state preparation, channel fluctuation. The core: how far can practical BB84 deviate from its ideal assumptions before security or key-generation performance becomes unreliable? And can we tell real noise from adversarial disturbance — is a rising QBER hardware imperfection, or an attack?

BB84QKDSecurity
OP-17

Can BB84 dynamically adapt to changing channel conditions?

Looking for Collaborators

A real quantum channel isn't fixed — loss and noise vary over time, so static settings are either too conservative or unstable. Can a BB84 system adapt its operating parameters — error correction, privacy amplification, transmission rate — in real time without weakening its security guarantees? If ML is involved, it is cleaner to use it for channel estimation and parameter optimization rather than letting it make security decisions directly.

BB84Adaptive SystemsQuantum Networks
OP-18

How should BB84 coexist with classical communication in hybrid networks?

Looking for Collaborators

BB84 usually handles only key distribution while the actual data travels on a classical channel. From a systems view: how should quantum key distribution and classical communication be jointly designed as one reliable system? How do we match key-generation rate to classical traffic demand, degrade gracefully when the QKD link falters, allocate quantum-key resources across many users and links, and optimize end-to-end latency and security together?

BB84Hybrid CommunicationNetwork Systems

Large Language Models (LLM)

OP-19

Can LLMs know when they are wrong?

Looking for Collaborators

LLMs generate answers with high confidence, but that confidence often fails to match actual correctness. Can an LLM reliably estimate when its own answer is likely to be wrong? Beyond token probability, can we measure semantic uncertainty, flag hallucination risk before answering, and keep calibration under domain shift?

LLMUncertaintyTrustworthy AI
OP-20

How can LLMs remain reliable under adversarial or misleading context?

Looking for Collaborators

LLMs are easily swayed by prompt injection, misleading context, and poisoned retrieval results. Can an LLM distinguish trustworthy instructions and evidence from adversarial or misleading context? Can injection be caught at the representation level, can a RAG system judge the reliability of retrieved documents, can correct reasoning survive partly-malicious context, and can defenses generalize to new attacks?

LLMSecurityRobustness
OP-21

Can we understand why an LLM changes its answer?

Looking for Collaborators

A tiny change to a prompt can sharply change the answer or reasoning — so what actually changed in the internal representation? Can we identify the internal representation changes that cause an LLM to switch its reasoning or final answer? Do semantically-equal prompts induce similar internal representations, can certain layers or attention patterns predict answer instability, and can representation geometry or topology explain reasoning transitions? A natural Topology × LLM bridge.
