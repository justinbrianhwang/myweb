About Me

👋 Hi, I'm

Sunjun Brian Hwang

an independent researcher in Vancouver, building on my undergraduate research at Yonsei University's RAISE Lab. What pulls me across so many fields — quantum computing, AI security, federated learning, autonomous driving — is the same gap each time: how a system is supposed to behave, and how it actually behaves under noise, adversarial pressure, hardware faults, or real-world constraints. That gap is where my research lives.

The Researcher

My Research Journey

I started in Computer Science at Yonsei in 2022 and joined the RAISE Lab in December 2024, working on reliable AI and quantum computing. After graduating in August 2026, I moved to Vancouver and kept the same research going as an independent researcher.

CS, with a Physics Core

My degree is in Computer Science, but I planned to double in Physics and took the courses that mattered most to me — Quantum Mechanics I & II among them. Physics gives me a vocabulary for uncertainty; CS lets me build with it. Quantum ML sits right where the two meet.

What I Want to Solve

  1. The gap between how a system is supposed to behave and how it actually behaves.
  2. Whether we can trust that system at all — under noise, attacks, or hardware faults.
  3. How to improve it once we know where it breaks.
  4. And in quantum, how to design it optimally from the start.

Theory ↔ Implementation

Every algorithm I study, I want to see running — on an FPGA, on a real NISQ device, in a federated learning client. Theory without implementation is incomplete; implementation without theory is brittle.

How I Approach Failure

When an experiment of mine fell apart, I was honestly discouraged. So I stepped away — took a trip, emptied my head, and came back properly refreshed. Then I changed direction, redesigned the experiment, and that version worked. Now I treat a failed run as a signal to rethink the design, not to push harder on the same one.

I Document Obsessively

This site is one of several I maintain — a CV, a research portal, a Wiki, guides, paper reviews, and a conference tracker, all in the sidebar. Half of them started as notes to my future self.

The Person

Why I Chose Research

No million-dollar business idea has ever come to me. But I have the room to think — and with that room, research is the most interesting thing I can imagine doing. I get to chase questions instead of revenue.

The Cat in the Favicon

Because Schrödinger. Quantum mechanics was the first time physics felt both rigorous and beautifully strange — a system that is neither this nor that until you look. The cat on every tab is a daily reminder of what got me here.

Small Joys

Watching a younger sibling or junior happily eat a meal I bought. Seeing a junior finally crack a problem they asked me about a week earlier. The small wins are the real ones.

Let's Talk

Why this line stuck with me: I first ran into it when I was deciding whether to push a research direction that nobody around me believed in.
